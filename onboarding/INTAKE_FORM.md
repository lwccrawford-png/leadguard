# LeadGuard Client Intake Form

Two sources feed a new client's setup, and they're split deliberately:

- **Part A (this form)** — hard facts that are quick and accurate to type. Send this before
  the onboarding call so it's already done by the time you talk.
- **Part B (the onboarding call)** — everything that comes out more naturally in
  conversation than in a form field: tone, real FAQs, the exceptions and edge cases a client
  will mention offhand but never think to write down unprompted. If the onboarding call is on
  Zoom, run the transcript through `TRANSCRIPT_EXTRACTION_PROMPT.md` afterward instead of
  re-listening for this — see that file.

Fill in the dashboard's Settings/Knowledge tabs from the combined result of both.

---

## Part A — send as a form (Google Form/Typeform)

1. **Business name** (exactly as you want it to appear to visitors):

2. **Website URL** (for the site crawl):

3. **Hours of operation**:

4. **Service area** (city/region, "nationwide," "remote only," etc.):

5. **Scheduling link** — your Calendly/Cal.com/Acuity (or similar) link. If you don't have
   one yet, leave blank and we'll set one up:

6. **Where should new leads go?** — a Slack channel, an email address, or a CRM — whatever
   you already use for following up with inquiries:

7. **Should the assistant have a name**, or just be introduced as "the [Business] assistant"?

---

## Part B — covered by the onboarding call (extract from transcript, don't re-ask)

These are still useful as a checklist for you to make sure the conversation actually covers
them — just don't make the client type long answers to them in a form.

8. **One or two sentences describing what you do** (used to shape the assistant's persona).
9. **Pricing** — a range, a starting price, or "quote only, don't discuss numbers"?
10. **Top 5–8 questions customers ask most often** — and the answer to each. These become
    the assistant's FAQs.
11. **Anything the assistant should never say or promise** (compliance/legal boundaries,
    guarantees not to make, competitors not to discuss).
12. **Tone/personality** — how the business wants to come across.
13. **When the assistant can't answer something**, how should it hand off — phone call, text,
    email, or "just capture the info and we'll reach out"?

---

If there's no onboarding call for a given client (pure async setup), fall back to asking
Part B as form questions too — the split above is the fast path, not the only path.
